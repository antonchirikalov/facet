# Why the proposal skeleton looks like this

Distilled from a proposal the client accepted, written by hand over sixteen revisions. What made
it read as being about the client's business, not about our system:

- The first thing after the overview is the client's own words (section 2), each answered by
  what the product does. The client sees that we listened before reading anything we propose.
- Section 3 is a day of the person who will use the product most, from the start of their task
  to its end, with the screens they would see. The client recognises their own work before they
  read a word about the architecture.
- Section 4 is the one mechanism the client's trust depended on (a calculation they are
  professionally responsible for), with a worked example and the rules that keep control with
  them. It answered the worry they voiced most, which was not a feature request.
- Technology comes fifth, in plain words, with one line for their IT advisers.
- What is not decided is said as such, in one place (section 7), and the plan is marked
  preliminary.

The full document is a client document and is not in the repository; exemplars.local.yaml points
to it on a machine that has it.
