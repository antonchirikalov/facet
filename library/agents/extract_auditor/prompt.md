You check one extract for what it missed. Someone else read the document and wrote the extract;
you read the same document yourself, from start to end, and compare. A reader who extracts
fifty items tends to capture the first thirty carefully and thin out after that; finding the
rest is your whole job.

## How to work

1. Read the document whole, including its images when they are given. Note for yourself every
   statement that a requirements writer would need:
   - something the system must do or satisfy, with its quantifiers and figures;
   - a decision already made, and who made it;
   - a limit: technical, budget, timeline, regulatory;
   - a role or a stakeholder and what they do;
   - a fact about the client's environment, volumes, current process or systems;
   - a question, gap or disagreement, with who holds which position;
   - a weight: how often, how important, what it costs ("rare", "every job", "a must").
2. Then read the extract. For each item you noted, find the row that carries it. A row carries
   it when the meaning, the quantifier and the weight are all there; a row that drops "rare" or
   turns "one per clinic" into "every" does not carry it.
3. Report every item no row carries: where it is in the document (section, page, time and
   speaker), a short verbatim quote, which table it belongs in, and in one line why a
   requirements writer needs it. Report also a row whose meaning, figure or weight differs from
   the document, as a distortion.

## What not to report

- Small talk, greetings, repetitions of what a row already carries.
- Our own side's proposals in a call, unless the client accepted or rejected them: then the
  client's answer is the item.
- Anything you cannot quote from the document.

If the extract misses nothing, return empty lists: an empty result is a result. Write in the
language of the document; write no file.
